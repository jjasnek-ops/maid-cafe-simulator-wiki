## What are the full-game level caps?

The developer's [launch announcement](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1846018067928563) sets the **cafe level cap at 40** and the **maid level cap at 15**. The demo cafe cap is 10. A demo limit is not evidence of a full-game progression bug.

| System | Full-game information confirmed at launch |
|---|---|
| Main story | 13 chapters in the Steam store feature list |
| Cafe level | Maximum 40 |
| Maid level | Maximum 15 |
| Roster | Five maids; Yuki, Jennie and Momo join as introduced by the main story |
| Festivals | 13 festivals rotating through the calendar |
| Maidchelin | Judge visits after requirements are met |
| Dance Stage | Live performances during shifts, with additional tips |
| Performance Stage | Filming every fifth night |

## Which upgrades does the full game add?

The announcement confirms main-hall and kitchen expansions, a brewer, professional cooking stations and a second register. These additions expand what you can do in the cafe. It does not provide a complete cost table, an optimal purchase order or a numerical worker-capacity formula.

Use the upgrade requirement shown in your active objective. If your problem is specifically a fifth maid who cannot work, see the [staff-capacity community report](/guides/troubleshooting/#why-can-you-not-put-the-fifth-maid-to-work). That report supports checking the main-hall expansion; it does not independently establish an exact level threshold.

For the underlying order, receipt and supply loop, start with the [beginner walkthrough](/guides/walkthrough/).

## How do Maidchelin visits and three stars work?

The developer says judges visit when their requirements are met, and Steam publishes a **Three Stars** achievement. That confirms the rating goal, but does not supply every condition for each star. The store's description of the ambition to reach three stars is not a numerical scoring formula.

At launch, a [Mission 6/7 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3232690/discussions/0/581684470096302169/) identified a missed nighttime conversation outside the cafe before a reviewer visit. It is a community check for that particular situation, not a universal three-star route. The [troubleshooting guide](/guides/troubleshooting/) explains the evidence and its limits.

## What is the difference between the two stages?

| Stage | Published role | Published timing |
|---|---|---|
| Dance Stage | Maids perform during a shift and generate extra tips | During cafe work |
| Performance Stage | Film a performance, choosing maid, outfit, song difficulty and target shots; take the post-performance photo | Every fifth night |

Treat these as separate activities. A request involving the performance camera or promo photo is not necessarily referring to a Dance Stage shift. The launch materials do not publish score thresholds or the exact controls for each shot.

## What should you know about festivals and sales?

The launch announcement confirms thirteen rotating festivals. The **Peak Season** achievement requires $1,000 earned on a single festival day, while **There's Money in This** tracks $50,000 in cumulative sales. Those counters are different: neither description establishes an optimal daily-profit target.

The [achievement table](/reference/achievements/) lists these objectives alongside service and collection milestones. No festival-by-festival profit ranking is claimed here because it would require verified prices, costs and demand data.
